# Google Maps Platform as a paid dependency in the booking hot path

Travel-time-aware scheduling needs to know where a job is and how long it takes to drive there, and `Booking.customerAddress` is `varchar(512)` of whatever a customer typed into a chat window. We are taking on **Google Geocoding (v3) + Routes (Compute Route Matrix)** on one platform-held API key, called synchronously while a customer waits for slots. This reverses the carve-out recorded at `docs/booking-epic-gap-analysis.md:195` and again at `:38`, where a distance-derived travel buffer was twice deliberately declined as *"blocked on geocoding this platform deliberately does not have"*.

**The rejected alternative was free, and it was rejected on correctness, not cost.** Belgium's municipality table already ships in `api/src/contracts/belgium-geo.data.json` (562 records, bpost/BOSA via Opendatasoft, CC-BY 4.0), so open-data centroids plus a calibrated speed model would have cost nothing, needed no API, and made this ADR unnecessary along with most of ADR-0015. It fails on one point: a centroid collapses every address in a municipality onto a single dot, so two jobs on opposite sides of Antwerp read as zero distance and auto-confirm a thirty-minute drive — precisely the defect the feature exists to prevent. Coarse positions can raise an alarm; they cannot clear one. That asymmetry survives into ADR-0015 as the degraded-mode rule, so the cheap path is not wasted, it is demoted to a fallback. A second alternative, self-hosted OSM routing (OSRM/Valhalla), was not evaluated in the research and is the obvious re-entry point if the burdens below become intolerable.

**What we are knowingly buying.** Google's terms are default-deny on caching — *"Customer will not cache Google Maps Content except as expressly permitted"* (§3.2.3(b)) — and the Maps Service Specific Terms permit `place_id` indefinitely, latitude/longitude for **30 consecutive calendar days**, and computed durations **at no TTL at all** (§19.3 enumerates lat/lng and nothing else; §11.8 grants duration explicitly for a different API, so the omission is deliberate). Three consequences follow and none of them are optional. `place_id` is the durable identity on a booking and lat/lng is a ≤30-day derived cache with a real deletion job. That deletion collides with a 60-day default `maxHorizonDays` (`booking-providers/service-timing.ts`), so re-resolving coordinates from `place_id` before a far-future appointment is the **normal** path, not an edge case. And durations may only be cached for the life of a conversation, which is defensible rather than expressly permitted — the research found no clause anywhere distinguishing Redis from a database, so this is a deliberate short reach documented as such, not a licence we hold.

Also accepted: a second platform-wide single point of failure alongside `OPENAI_API_KEY`, whose blast radius ADR-0015 exists to contain, and unbudgeted Google attribution UI with specified logo sizes and fonts — required on *every* surface showing a drive time, because we display them off a Google map.

**The EEA terms govern.** The payments profile country is Belgium, and the pre-July-2025 grandfather carve-out does not apply because enabling new APIs modifies the integration. Caching is word-for-word identical across both documents (EEA §6.2.1/§6.2.2 and §20.2), so nothing in the data model moves; the difference is that EEA §6.1 permits lat/lng and `place_id` to be shown *With any Map*, and §20.1 restricts only Routes *descriptions and steps*. A future "map of the owner's day" may therefore use Leaflet or OSM rather than an embedded Google Map, which the standard terms would have forbidden (§6.2/§19.2 and ToS §3.2.3(e)). **That permission rides on a mutable billing address** — moving the billing account out of the EEA would silently make such a screen non-compliant, so it is a constraint to re-check on any billing change rather than a settled property of the product.

**One operational precondition, currently unmet:** the key must sit on an upgraded, non-trial billing account. Today's is a free trial, and a lapsed trial is not a clean failure — it is ADR-0015's Routes-unreachable branch firing permanently and silently for every tenant at once. This is gate 5 in the plan's rollout, not a deployment detail.

Build on Geocoding **v3**: v4 went GA on 2026-03-30 and removed both hard `components` country filtering and `partial_match`, which are exactly the controls that make a `ROOFTOP` result trustworthy. Cost was explicitly ruled a non-factor by the founder, but note that `departureTime` is request-level on Route Matrix, so traffic-aware pricing cannot batch distinct departure times into one matrix — only traffic-unaware lookups and common-time buckets batch. Traffic-aware inside ~24h, traffic-unaware beyond.

**No incident prompted this.** As of 2026-08-06 no owner has been recorded standing in the wrong city and no booking is known to have been lost to an impossible drive; the refreshed scorecard still marks travel time as deliberately out. The feature is being built on reasoning, not evidence, by an explicit founder decision. A future reader wondering why a paid external dependency entered the booking hot path with no reported defect behind it is asking a fair question, and this paragraph is the answer.
