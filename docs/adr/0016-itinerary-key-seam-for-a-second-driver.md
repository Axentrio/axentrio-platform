# Travel scopes to an itinerary key, so a second driver is rows and not a migration

Travel feasibility is a claim about one person's day: *given where they finish, can they reach where they must start next?* Nothing in `api/src/database/entities/` models a person — there is no staff, technician or resource entity — so today one Agent is one diary is one implicit driver, and the feature is only coherent under that assumption. The founder expects to handle more than one person on the road eventually but not now, so the requirement is a **seam**, not a resource model: the second driver must arrive as extra rows rather than as a schema change to everything travel touches.

We pass an abstract **`itineraryKey`** through the travel path — neighbour loading, feasibility assertion, and the enable check — currently equal to `calendar_key` and produced by `resolveItineraryKey(botId)` in `scheduler/itinerary-key.ts`, which delegates to the existing `conflictKeyFor(botId, identity, provider)` in `scheduler/calendar-rekey.ts` rather than computing the key a second way. `calendar_key` is already the right *current* answer to "whose day is this": it normalises to the connected calendar's account-unique identity so that bots pointed at one real calendar share a key, it is what the advisory lock takes (`pg_advisory_xact_lock` in `internal.provider.ts`) and what `InternalBookingProvider.loadBusy` filters on, and as of `d2851c4` it is also what the Minimum Gap check uses. Two drivers then means two diaries, which the system already models. The seam exists so that when a driver becomes a first-class thing, `resolveItineraryKey` changes rather than every call site: availability, create, request, accept and reschedule each resolve the key once and hand it to the helpers that scope by it, so none of them re-derives a diary identity of its own. The stored column keeps its name — the key is what that column *means* to travel, not a new source of truth, and renaming it would be a migration in exchange for nothing.

**Settings stay bot-owned and must not move onto the key.** This was the tempting mistake and it is a real bug: `rekeyBotBookings` (`calendar-rekey.ts:38`) rewrites `calendar_key` on a bot's future bookings whenever the owner connects, switches or disconnects a calendar, so anything hung on the key is orphaned by an ordinary settings change. `BookingSettings` is per-bot and stays there. The key scopes *enforcement*; the bot owns *configuration*. Correspondingly, the two halves of `enforceBusinessCapacity` scope differently on purpose — day ceilings ask what this business sold today and count by `bot_id`, the gap and travel ask what is parked nearby in the diary and scope by the key — which is the substance of `d2851c4`.

**Two bots sharing one key is the multi-driver case arriving early by accident**, and the per-bot travel toggle must refuse to enable in that state rather than gate slots against drives nobody makes: under a shared key the two bots' bookings are one itinerary to this feature, and a two-plumber business would find slots stripped for journeys neither of them takes, leaving them worse off than before the feature existed. `rekeyBotBookings` already surfaces the adjacent hazard as `CALENDAR_REKEY_CONFLICT`, so shared-key eligibility is re-checked there too — a rekey can create the shared state after travel was legitimately enabled. Known and accepted gap: when two bots do share a key and have different `minGapMin` values, the booking bot's number governs the shared diary. That is monotonically safer than what shipped before `d2851c4` but it is not complete, and whose gap wins on a shared diary is a product question deliberately left open.
